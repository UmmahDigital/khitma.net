Khitma.net

Complete the Quran together

Khitma.net is a free web app for group khitmas. Start one, share its link with family and friends, and each person picks a juz to read. Everyone sees what has been read, what is being read and what is still free, until the khitma is complete.

Start a khitma Create a group that completes the Quran regularly

How it works

  1. Start a khitma: give it a title and write your name. For someone who passed away, for someone who is ill, for Ramadan, or for any intention.
  2. Share the invitation in a WhatsApp group, or let the people around you scan its QR code.
  3. Each person opens the link, writes their name and picks one of the 30 juz. No account and nothing to install.
  4. When they have read it, they tap “I finished reading juz”. Everyone sees the progress until the khitma is complete.

Made for reading together

For someone who passed away

The khitma's title is written for them, and when it is complete, the supplication for the deceased appears for everyone to say.

Khitmas that repeat

A group can start a new khitma every day, every Friday or every month, by itself, and each member gets the juz after theirs.

Nobody is forgotten

A reminder in your calendar for the juz you took, and a ready message asking for help with the ajza that are still free.

Together in one place

In a house of condolence or a gathering, show the khitma on a big screen with a QR code to scan, and watch the ajza fill up.

On any phone

It works in the browser, on older phones too, with large text and dark mode, and groups you opened before open even without a connection.

Questions

What is a khitma?

A khitma (also written khatam or khatm) is a complete reading of the Quran, from beginning to end. In a group khitma, the 30 parts of the Quran (ajza, singular juz) are shared out, so that together the group completes the whole Quran.

Is Khitma.net free?

Yes, completely free. Participants don't need an account or an app: they open the link and write their name.

How long does a group khitma take?

It depends on the group: with 30 people each reading one juz, a khitma can be completed in a day. When you start one, you can choose a deadline: three days, a week, by Friday, or another date.

What if someone takes a juz and doesn't read it?

The organiser can free the ajza taken a week or more ago, so that others can take them, and can mark a juz as read on someone's behalf.

Can our group complete the Quran every week?

Yes. A recurring khitma can start again by itself every day, every Friday or every month, and each member automatically gets the juz after the one they read.

Who can see the khitma?

Only people who have its link. Khitma pages are kept out of search engines, because their titles often carry the names of loved ones.
